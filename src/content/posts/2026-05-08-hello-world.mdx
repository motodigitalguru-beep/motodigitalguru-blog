This is the first post.

I'm writing here because the last few months have been mostly spent on a
question I can't get out of my head: **what does engineering actually look
like when one human and a capable AI ship things together?**

I'm not a developer. I'd describe myself as someone who can hold a complex
system in their head, who can spot when a thing is structurally off, who
can decide what *not* to build. The tactical layer — typing, syntax,
boilerplate — I delegate. To Claude, mostly. The strategic layer — what
problem is this even solving, who pays, what breaks at scale, what does
the law require — stays mine.

That split used to be uncommon. With AI as a serious co-pilot, I think
it's becoming a normal mode of operating, especially for solo founders
and small teams. This blog will be a build log of that mode: real
projects, real diagnoses, real moments where I overruled the AI and was
right, and the moments where I should have overruled it sooner.

A few things I expect to write about:

- Build logs from active projects (more on those soon)
- Diagnoses — patterns where AI tools quietly produce subtly wrong things,
  and how to design around it
- System thinking without code — the part that doesn't go away when the
  models get better
- Decisions: when to automate, when to override, when to ship anyway

A note on language. English isn't my first language. I'm writing here
partly to think and partly to get better at it. If something reads
slightly off — that's the price of doing this in public. If you have a
correction or a counter-argument, I'd rather hear it than not.

More soon.
